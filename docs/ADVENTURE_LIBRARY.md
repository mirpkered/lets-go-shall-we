# Adventure Library Status

Current registry: **637 playable adventures**, verified against `src/scenarios/index.ts`; this includes ten fixed-stock merchant encounters and seven corrective Gear-expansion batches. The detailed table below is a historical 175-adventure inventory snapshot, not a complete current roster; later additions are summarized in [Scenario Diversity](SCENARIO-DIVERSITY.md), and the full stable-ID registry is `src/scenarios/index.ts`. Current primary-category, risk, season, and historical-metadata counts are in [Current Systems](CURRENT_SYSTEMS.md). Stable IDs preserve saves and should not be renamed casually.

The Deep Exploration lane adds fourteen all-year, `EPIC_SHORT` adventures using the existing forward-only scenario graph, selector, QA launch list, reward resolver, and save/resume state. They intentionally vary between machinery descent, overlapping houses, rescue-then-optional-depth, a cross-door road system, water-state exploration, respectful burial survey, a clue-led cave maze, and historical service routes. Their stable IDs are in `src/scenarios/deepExplorationBatch.ts`; the internal authoring targets are in [Design Rules](DESIGN_RULES.md). Scene counts are not promised play times; individual routes range from short retreat/shallow successes to deeper multi-branch resolutions.

The route figures above are maximum graph paths, not typical observed session time. Each graph was checked for forward-only acyclicity, orphan scenes, missing destinations, actionable states, and a gearless alternative at every reachable choice scene. Shared labels such as “branching narrative” are the canonical metadata vocabulary; each story’s more specific structure lives in its distinguishing hook and the route graph, not a parallel schema.

The surprise anthology adds 36 individually authored, all-year stories after a library-gap review. It strengthens underrepresented competition/performance, communication, social participation, caregiving, and everyday information/play without creating a new selection system, structured contact roster, or carryable-item set. New graphs use the existing shared selector, history/knowledge, money, and inventory rules where the fiction supports them.

The comedy/absurdity batch adds 25 all-year, low-risk, grounded stories with distinct physical, procedural, social, animal, craft, and public-event engines. The six standing comedy rules were already in the design guide and were preserved. A manual candidate-by-candidate overlap record, including two dropped concepts, is in [Comedy / Absurdity Overlap Audit](COMEDY-ABSURDITY-OVERLAP-AUDIT.md).

The remote-discovery/frontier-claims addition contributes 40 all-year adventures. It broadens the existing exploration family with environmental discoveries, hidden occupancy, claim evidence, salvage choices, and structural hazards. Most start from something the traveler notices rather than an NPC request. The collection uses current Knowledge, History, and money only; it does not assume structured Contacts/Favors or add new persistent items. See the source records in `src/scenarios/frontierDiscoveryBatch.ts` and graph authoring helper in `frontierDiscoveryTools.ts`.

This is an inventory, not a claim that all adventures have received hands-on playtesting. Broad category is inferred from the authoring module; tone and danger vary by route and should be read in the story itself. Approximate shortest/typical route lengths and payoff/reward/quiet signals mirror the structural scan recorded in the [ending/reward audit](LIBRARY-ENDING-REWARD-AUDIT.md), not a newly recomputed analysis; that report states its baseline and measurement limits. “Material” means a positive money or carryable-item reward was authored in the scanned version. “Character-state callback potential” means history/lore/knowledge effects were detected, not that a future cross-adventure callback exists. No unresolved issue is asserted by this inventory.
