# Canonical Design Rules

These rules govern new adventures and focused revisions. They are consolidated from the project’s authoring notes; scenario data and tests remain the authority for what is implemented. The [World Continuity Appendix](WORLD-CONTINUITY.md) adds detailed callback and provenance guidance without replacing these rules.

## Traveler, era, and continuity

- **Traveler Narrative Framework:** The persistent character travels from place to place, takes opportunities as they come, and may help, refuse, or move on. Vary how a story begins; do not assume a guild, rank, profession, or universal fame. Every adventure must make sense for a fresh traveler. Relevant history may flavor dialogue or unlock an optional approach, but must not gate basic comprehension or success.
- **Period Consistency Rule:** Use the fictional pre-modern world inspired primarily by the late 19th century. Preserve its broad transport, communication, work, medical, and social texture; avoid modern technology and emergency assumptions.
- **Historical Flexibility Rule:** This is not an exact-year simulation. Avoid clear anachronisms, but favor readability and broad period fit over uncertain adoption-date trivia. Keep technology, terminology, medicine, economy, and infrastructure internally plausible.
- **Historical Cameo Rule:** Historical people and events are rare, optional points of reference—not a history-simulator system or a reason to anchor the independent fictional world to an exact year or geography. Keep encounters grounded and brief; distinguish established fact, contemporary reputation, and later legend, and label a portrayal as grounded, legendary, or mixed. A cameo is not a new adventure requirement and should not be forced into callbacks.
- **Continuity Must Have Teeth:** Across the library, choices can affect health, life, money, property, gear, opportunity, and relationships. Not every story needs danger; do not make persistent continuity decorative or protect players from clearly understood risks.
- **Current-Run Knowledge Rule / Knowledge-Aware Narration:** The traveler knows only what this living character has observed, been told, or learned. Player memory and dead/retired characters’ knowledge do not transfer. Gate private identities, clues, motives, and fates accordingly.
- **History Is Not Morality:** Character history records specific behavior and relevant discoveries, not fame, karma, alignment, heroism, or faction rank. Use selective, plausible callbacks and provide a natural first-meeting version.
- **NPC Name Variety Rule:** Inspect the existing roster before naming new people. Prefer unused names; Eli and Mara are already common and should not be reused casually. Reuse is welcome when recurrence is intentional and explicit. Do not rename established characters mechanically unless a duplicate causes actual confusion.
- **Important Context / No Opaque Shorthand Rule:** Refer back to earlier events in short natural language that reconnects the player to the fact. Avoid phrases that sound like unexplained jargon or a new concept.

## Fair choices and consequences

- **Fair Consequence Rule:** Foreshadow serious risks through the environment, visible danger, prior knowledge, or testimony. A bad choice may carry consequences, but should not become an arbitrary trap.
- **Risk Is Not Certainty:** A risky action should usually retain a plausible chance of success. Do not treat risk as guaranteed failure unless the action is physically impossible and that impossibility is clear.
- **Consequential Risk Rule:** Fairness is not safety. A clearly telegraphed action may cause severe injury, death, failed rescue, loss, or financial cost. Do not shield a character from a danger they knowingly choose.
- **Lethal Consequence Rule:** Death is appropriate when physically plausible and caused by the authored hazard, choices, and escalation. Foreshadow or make the danger inferable; never add arbitrary one-click fatality to inflate difficulty. Sensible choices can retain inherent risk, but danger never guarantees death.
- **Long-Run Pressure Rule:** Surviving travelers should encounter a varied mix that continues to include meaningful danger. Apply pressure through authored stakes and soft adventure-selection weighting only—not altered odds, hidden enemy scaling, reduced stats, or streak punishment.
- **Internal Risk Classification:** Classify scenarios LOW (primarily inconvenience/social/material stakes), MODERATE (injury or serious loss possible), HIGH (meaningful lethal branches with mitigation/retreat often available), or SEVERE (survival is central and sensible choices may still be lethal). Tier follows actual consequence, not genre or tone. Every tier remains eligible; selection weights are not guarantees.
- **Choice-Driven Conflict Rule:** Resolve combat and confrontation through choices shaped by position, environment, equipment, knowledge, wounds, timing, retreat, and chance where appropriate. Do not add classes, attack stats, mana, levels, or a traditional turn-by-turn combat loop.
- **Retreat Has a Price Rule:** Retreat often remains possible, but may cost the objective, an NPC, money, gear, property, reputation, or opportunity. Do not make retreat universally safe or universally punished; clearly show any serious risk.
- **Owned-Asset Jeopardy Rule:** A character-bound owned asset may be endangered only through explicit, state-aware story events with a clear opportunity to protect or accept the loss. Never delete owned property randomly or silently.
- **Explicit Loss Rule:** Threatened carried gear, money, transport, wages, property, or relationships may be lost only through a legible authored consequence. Narrate what is at risk and whether it was lost, damaged, protected, or abandoned.
- **Known Problem Rule:** Establish the actual problem, why it matters, and the relevant stakes before asking for a decision. Important constraints should be repeated naturally when they affect a later choice.
- **No-Win Situation Rule:** Some situations cannot be made perfect. Success may mean reducing harm, saving one person or possession, helping partially, or leaving safely. Do not invent a perfect route to satisfy game expectations.
- **No Preferred-Answer Framing:** Present legitimate options and their tradeoffs consistently. Do not signal a hidden “correct” answer through loaded wording or unequal descriptions.
- **Conditional Incident Rule:** A central crisis need not occur if the player stays outside the circumstances that trigger it.
- **Non-Inevitable Incident Rule:** Participating in an activity should not guarantee a dramatic incident when quieter outcomes are plausible. Persist the authored run outcome so reloads do not reroll it.
- **Distant Intervention Rule:** When direct access is blocked, consider meaningful indirect actions—warning, signaling, distraction, recruiting help, or preparation—with plausible partial success and failure.
- **Mystery Motivation Rule:** Before inviting investigation, establish what is unusual, why the traveler might care, what is known, and what remains uncertain.

## Spatial, physical, and item logic

- **Spatial Clarity / Physical Setup Rule:** Before a decision depends on positions, routes, barriers, vehicles, animals, hazards, or connections, establish them in ordinary language. Narrate movement explicitly; do not teleport people or objects, or rely on unseen equipment.
- **Physical Evidence Rule:** Clues must be observable under the scene’s actual weather, light, distance, water, and viewpoint. Do not assume tracks, sounds, or objects could be perceived when conditions prevent it.
- **Important-Detail Reinforcement Rule:** Repeat a decision-critical fact when it affects a choice after a branch or delay. Keep the reminder natural rather than tutorial-like.
- **Equipment Accessibility Rule:** Carried inventory is not automatically in hand. Respect gear set aside, left behind, inaccessible, wet, damaged, or impractical in the current position.
- **Persistent Equipment Evolution Rule:** Persistent gear may remain sound, become damaged or broken through an explicitly narrated strain, be repaired, receive a named functional upgrade, be explicitly replaced, or be intentionally discarded. Do not add passive degradation, hidden durability meters, arbitrary wear, or automatic replacement. Broken gear remains owned but does not satisfy usable-equipment requirements; repair is available only where the fiction provides it. Upgrades are item-specific, preserve the item’s identity and carry slot, and have a small authored cap (normally two or fewer). Bank deposit/withdrawal, death, and retirement preserve or lose equipment state according to the same rules as the item itself: Banked gear survives; unbanked gear and its state do not survive a lost traveler. See [Equipment Evolution Audit](EQUIPMENT-EVOLUTION-AUDIT.md).
- **Short Does Not Mean Nonqualifying:** A brief adventure qualifies when the traveler meaningfully resolves its objective or makes a persistent carried-gear or money change; length alone is not a progression gate. Explicit trivial refusals may be marked non-substantive. Responsible routes should receive fitting acknowledgment and payoff without manufactured danger.
- **Tool Capability Rule:** Use tools only for tasks their shape and strength support. State what the tool acts on and what it can achieve; avoid generic unexplained bonuses.
- **Employer-Supplied Tools Rule:** When asked to do ordinary work, the traveler should normally be given or lent the employer’s basic tools and materials. Do not sell an employer’s ordinary supplies back to the traveler; carried gear may offer a distinct safer or faster method.
- **NPC Information, Player Decision Rule:** NPCs explain available resources, facts, limits, and risks. The player chooses the strategy; avoid having an NPC enumerate the solution menu.
- **No Invisible Important Rewards:** Important items and advantages require an explicit choice, narrated handoff, or visible discovery. Never silently grant them.
- **Recognizable Property Provenance:** Record how distinctive property was acquired when the traveler knowingly keeps it. Future recognition may invite a grounded reaction, not automatic punishment or a morality score.
- **Intentional Disposal Rule:** Never silently overwrite, discard, or replace persistent items. Require an explicit, understandable player choice; disposal should be clearly described and confirmed where appropriate.
- **Reward Storage Rule:** At an authored ending, an earned persistent item may be carried within capacity, stored directly in available Bank space, or declined. A choose-one reward stays choose-one. Full carry plus full Bank must have a safe explicit resolution; ending reward placement is not full Bank management.
- **Supernatural Item Rule:** Strange items may have narrow atmospheric effects, not universal detection, keys, or combat upgrades. Do not force every relic into every story.
- **Inventory Class Rule:** Every catalog item has one explicit resolved class: Gear, Supply, Relic, Asset, or Temporary. Gear uses the traveler’s 1/2/3 Gear Capacity and may have condition/upgrades; Relics are rare and separately listed; Supplies use quantity stacks; Assets remain character property; Temporary equipment ends with its adventure unless explicitly awarded.
- **Persistent Supply Rule:** Persist a consumable between adventures only when conserving, spending, replenishing, or losing it creates a meaningful future decision. Ordinary food, water, oil, string, nails, bandages, and matches stay abstract unless an authored design specifically justifies tracking them.
- **Limited-Use Item Rule:** Physically expended materials use quantity-based Supply stacks rather than infinite reusable-item behavior. Show consumption and remaining quantity to the player.
- **No Item Bloat Rule:** Do not create a persistent item merely to fill a reward slot. Prefer an existing item’s upgrade/discovered use, money, Supply replenishment, favor/contact, knowledge, history, access, repair, or property when it fits better.
- **Existing Relic First Rule:** Before adding a supernatural charm, token, key, lens, or ward, check whether an existing Relic can plausibly gain its function through discovered meaning, modification, repair, or preparation.
- **Occult Consumable Rule:** Occult materials physically expended by use—such as chalk, salt, herbs, nails, or wax—are limited-use Supplies with authored quantity and purpose, not infinite-use Gear.
- **Specific Protection Rule:** No Relic or occult Supply universally protects against supernatural threats. State its scope and limits; it may fail or be inapplicable.
- **Inventory Pressure Without Busywork:** Limited slots, distinct functions, consumable scarcity, gear condition, and character loss can create inventory tension. Do not require frequent sorting, weight arithmetic, or routine provision tracking.

## Story structure and endings

- **Forward-Only Story Rule:** A story screen is seen at most once in a run. Choices advance to new scenes; use a new convergence scene rather than returning to a prior screen.
- **Scene Value Rule:** Every scene changes the situation, reveals useful information, creates risk, pays off a choice, alters options, or advances the story. Remove or combine scenes that do none of these.
- **Content Substance Rule:** Do not end merely because the player performed the obvious procedural action. Usually show a meaningful consequence, reaction, performance result, escalation, discovery, complication, or payoff before completion. The ending should feel like the consequence of a story, not the end of a form.
- **Workday Substance Rule:** If an adventure centers on a job, the work itself should contain meaningful decisions or events, and the ending should reflect what the traveler actually accomplished. Use fitting dimensions such as amount/quality, pace, weather, equipment, injury, coworker help, time, and full/reduced/bonus pay; do not skip from starting work to a generic day-complete screen unless it is a deliberately tiny vignette with a strong payoff.
- **Witness / Information Payoff Rule:** When the player contributes testimony, observation, evidence, memory, or a neutral statement, show what it changes—what is clarified, narrowed, challenged, trusted, or still disputed. Recording the statement alone is usually not enough; uncertainty may remain.
- **Procedural Ending Rule:** Do not end immediately after an administrative action (reporting, submitting, comparing, handing over, returning property, waiting for an authority, or finishing an agreed task) unless that action itself produces a visible narrative payoff.
- **Performance Feedback Rule:** When the player performs measurable work, craft, labor, repair, or competition, often show how they did—output, quality, time, setback, bonus, injury, or another person’s reaction. Do not make it feel like pressing “Complete Job.”
- **Quiet Does Not Mean Empty Rule:** Peaceful, social, recreational, legal, domestic, and work-focused stories need no danger, but should still offer progression, reaction, consequence, personality, payoff, or a memorable detail. A happy quiet ending is welcome; an empty one is not.
- **Resolution Needs a Payoff Rule:** A substantial decision or central problem resolution should usually be followed by one concise consequence/aftermath beat before completion. Do not add a scene solely to pad length or progression.
- **Quiet Payoff Rule:** Peaceful and low-stakes paths still deserve fitting closure—a reaction, shared wait, ordinary follow-through, or departure can itself be the payoff.
- **No New Crisis Rule:** Payoff scenes close the existing situation; do not add danger just to extend the story.
- **State-Aware Narration / Ending Rule:** Prose must reflect the run’s actual actions, positions, successes, failures, injuries, rescues, losses, and available knowledge. Never narrate unused tools as used, failed warnings as successful, or unknown facts as known.
- **Known Fate Acknowledgment Rule:** When the traveler learned a specific person, animal, or important possession remained in a place later lost, acknowledge that known consequence. Preserve uncertainty when the current run never established the fact.
- **Rescue Diversity Rule:** Vary rescue methods to fit the hazard and geography; do not default every time to lifting or prying something off a trapped person.
- **Fresh-Traveler Viability:** No prior item, history, knowledge, or reward may be mandatory to understand or complete an adventure. Carry-over gear opens optional approaches.
- **No-Scroll Screen Rule:** At 320×720 and 390×844, fit the full scene, status, and choices without page scrolling. Test longest text variants and choice hints, preserve legibility and touch targets, and use meaningful forward beats rather than clipping or shrinking text.

## Systems and authoring constraints

- **No Scenario Factory Rule:** Do not mechanically reuse the same opening, scene count, choice rhythm, complication timing, decision pattern, climax, reward, or ending while swapping names and nouns. Batch work deliberately varies premise, role, pace, structure, risk, tone, objective, environment, complication, consequence, resolution, and payoff.
- **Library Uniqueness Rule:** Every adventure needs at least one memorable distinguishing feature beyond setting and names. The feature may be a physical constraint, social dynamic, decision structure, terrain-shaped fight, investigation, relationship, time pressure, consequence, reward provenance, or unusual ending; a new engine mechanic is not required.
- **Seasonal Availability Rule:** A calendar-bound adventure enters normal random selection only in its declared local-device month(s). Outside its window it remains in the registry and QA launch list; it is neither removed nor invalidated. Month gating applies only when starting a new normal run. A live adventure, earned reward, ownership, and history remain valid across date changes. Seasonal does not imply supernatural.
- **Scenario Diversity Metadata Rule:** Risk, fantasy density, combat, length, player role, activity, structure, tone, setting, entry, outcomes, rewards, consequences, season, and a distinctive hook are independent authoring/audit dimensions. Metadata measures variety; it is not a player-facing score or mandatory story template. Legacy classification may be inferred for review, but new scenarios should author distinctive values deliberately and correct inaccurate inferences.
- **Historical Presence Metadata Rule:** Classify historical presence independently as NONE, INSPIRED, CAMEO, FEATURED, or HISTORICAL_EVENT. Named figures/events and portrayal labels are recorded where applicable so the internal diversity report can reveal concentration or repetition; this metadata never changes normal selection or gameplay.

- **Traveler Progression Completion Rule:** An authored ending advances the living traveler’s completion count when it is marked substantive or resolves with changed persistent carried gear or money. Adventure length is not a gate; QA, abandonment, and explicitly non-substantive endings never qualify. See [Traveler Progression](TRAVELER-PROGRESSION.md).
- **Owned Property Rule:** Durable character-bound property (such as an animal kept at a farm) is visible separately from carried gear, consumes no carry slot, cannot be banked, and ends with its traveler.
- **Easter Egg Rule:** Rare events are optional flavor only. They never affect choices, odds, endings, inventory, money, history, knowledge, or progression; most runs should see none. Persist the event for resume and avoid undercutting serious scenes. They do not establish crossover canon.
- **Grounded Supernatural Rule:** Supernatural events are exceptional, quiet, and serious within the period-inspired world. Preserve ambiguity where intended; avoid routine fantasy classes, spell systems, or supernatural stat bonuses.
- **Risk-Weighted Selection Rule:** Normal selection excludes recent scenario IDs where the pool allows, then uses soft tier shares informed by traveler completion count and recent risk history. There is no fixed danger cadence; every eligible story can still be selected. See [Scenario Risk Audit](SCENARIO-RISK-AUDIT.md). Selection never changes an adventure’s internal odds.
- **Fictional Time Rule:** Decide which authored choices consume time, what thresholds change, and how urgency is communicated. Time advances only on choices, never while reading or while the app is closed. Use scenario-specific phases and thresholds where useful; avoid universal durations or speedrun rewards.
- **HUD Priority Rule:** Functional text, status, buttons, and controls must remain crisp and legible above atmospheric art. Use backplates/overlays as needed; do not blur functional UI.

For the practical pre-implementation and release checks, use the [Adventure Authoring Checklist](AUTHORING_CHECKLIST.md). The [World Continuity Appendix](WORLD-CONTINUITY.md) remains the detailed reference for locations, callbacks, and world persistence.
